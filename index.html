Free Read-Aloud Directory

Free text-to-speech and read-aloud helpers for readers who prefer listening — browser read modes, phone equivalents, free TTS apps, and e-readers with synthetic narration. Every entry lists its platform, whether it works offline, what to check before you rely on it, and a one-line plain-English verdict.

Independent, non-affiliated guide. Features change often — each entry tells you what to check so you can confirm it still works on your device. This is an accessibility/education resource, not medical or legal advice.

1. Browser read modes

If you are already using a modern browser, you may have a decent robot reader hiding in a menu. Start here — it is the cheapest win.

Microsoft Edge — Read Aloud (Immersive Reader)

  • Platform: Windows, macOS, Linux, iPad, Android
  • Offline: Partial — some voices download; natural voices generally need a connection
  • What it checks: Right-click the page or open the Reader view, then check voice quality, the speed slider (0.5× to 3×), word highlighting, and whether a natural-sounding voice is available for your language

The easiest free read-aloud on a laptop — right-click any page and hit “Read aloud.”

Google Chrome for Android — “Listen to this page”

  • Platform: Android (Chrome app)
  • Offline: Partial — prompted voices download to the phone
  • What it checks: Open any page, tap the three-dot menu, and look for “Listen to this page”; check language support (about 10 languages), playback speed, and that it works inside Reader mode

A surprisingly good one-tap reader built right into Chrome on your phone.

Apple Spoken Content — Speak Screen / Speak Selection

  • Platform: iPhone, iPad, macOS
  • Offline: Yes — built-in voices work with airplane mode
  • What it checks: Settings → Accessibility → Spoken Content; check “Speak Screen” (two-finger swipe from the top), word highlighting, the speech-rate dial, and nicer voices under “Voices” (some high-quality ones need one download)

A built-in, fully offline bed-time reader for anything on an iPhone or iPad.

Microsoft Word Immersive Reader — Read Aloud

  • Platform: Word on Windows, Mac, iPad, web (free Word Online)
  • Offline: Yes on desktop reads; Partial in the web version
  • What it checks: Review (or View) tab → Read Aloud; check voice choices, speed, and whether your paid/free Word account unlocks the same voices as Edge

Great for reading back your own documents without leaving Word.

2. Phone equivalents

The phone is where most people listen. These turn your pocket device into a free narration machine.

Google Play Books — “Listen” to your own e-books

  • Platform: Android, iOS, web
  • Offline: Partial — some auto-narration voices download
  • What it checks: Upload your own EPUB/PDF to Play Books, open it, tap “Listen”; check whether auto-narration is offered for that book, voice options, and page-turn highlighting

Turns a book you already own into an instant audiobook voiceover — for free.

Android Select to Speak (accessibility)

  • Platform: Android (Accessibility settings)
  • Offline: Yes — after the offline voice pack downloads
  • What it checks: Settings → Accessibility → Select to Speak; check tap-to-read on screens, camera capture (photograph text and hear it), and that your installed TTS voice offers offline download

Point your phone at a menu or letter and have it read to you — brilliant for print, too.

@Voice Aloud Reader (Android)

  • Platform: Android app
  • Offline: Yes — reads local files with an offline TTS engine
  • What it checks: Open EPUB, PDF, DOCX, TXT, or a web article; check the reading queue, background playback, speed, and whether your phone’s free TTS voice sounds acceptable (that’s the engine, not the app)

The old faithful “read my files out loud” Android app — free and surprisingly complete.

NaturalReader (free tier)

  • Platform: iOS, Android, web browser
  • Offline: No — the free tier is mostly online
  • What it checks: Free-plan limits (voice selection, words per session, PDF/DOCX upload rules); check it never asks for payment details to listen; skip paid plans unless you truly need premium voices

Smooth voices and a nice app, but treat the free tier as a testing ground, not a subscription trap.

3. Free TTS apps (computer, local, and web)

Tools that speak files or generate spoken audio, running on your own machine or in a free web tool.

Balabolka (Windows)

  • Platform: Windows (desktop, free)
  • Offline: Yes — fully offline with built-in Windows voices
  • What it checks: Open EPUB, PDF, DOCX, TXT and read or export audio files (MP3/WAV using compatible voices); check your Windows voice quality first — the app can only sound as good as the installed voices

No-frills, forever-free Windows workhorse that reads anything and can record it.

Piper TTS (open source, local)

  • Platform: Windows, macOS, Linux, Raspberry Pi (command line / hobbyist)
  • Offline: Yes — runs 100% on your own hardware
  • What it checks: Download a voice model, feed it text or text files; check model size vs. your hardware, speed on older machines, and that you are comfortable with a terminal (there is no pretty window)

Free, private, offline robot voices for tinkerers — the audio you make never leaves your home.

TTSMaker (free web TTS)

  • Platform: Web browser (any device)
  • Offline: No — everything happens in the browser tab
  • What it checks: Character limits per run, voice library by language, MP3/WAV download, and the usage terms for whatever you generate; check output before using it for anything public

Fast free text-to-audio with downloadable files — handy for small projects, not for whole books.

Microsoft Windows Narrator (built-in)

  • Platform: Windows (built-in screen reader)
  • Offline: Yes — installed with Windows
  • What it checks: Ctrl+Win+Enter to start it; check Narrator view (Caps+Space) for continuous reading of pages and PDFs, and adjust speed with Caps+Plus/Minus; it is a screen reader first, so browsing feels clunky

Clunky for casual listening, but zero-install and invaluable when nothing else is on the PC.

Audiobookshelf (self-hosted server)

  • Platform: Windows, macOS, Linux server + phone apps
  • Offline: Partial — download audiobooks for offline play; e-book narration is limited
  • What it checks: Run the free server on a spare PC or NAS, point it at your audiobook/e-book folders, then check the Android/iOS apps for offline listening, bookmarks, and sleep timer

For people with an audiobook collection and one spare computer — your private listening library.

4. E-readers with synthetic narration

A few e-ink readers (and the Kindle) can speak a book out loud with no phone required.

Amazon Kindle — VoiceView / Assistive Reader

  • Platform: Kindle e-readers (feature varies by model and region)
  • Offline: Yes — voices run on the device
  • What it checks: On your model, open Settings → Accessibility; check that VoiceView or Assistive Reader is present, works with Bluetooth earbuds, and reads the book being opened (some models only read menus)

Real synthetic narration straight from your Kindle — but check your exact model first, support varies wildly.

PocketBook — built-in TTS

  • Platform: Selected PocketBook e-readers (e.g., higher-end models)
  • Offline: Yes — everything on the device
  • What it checks: In an open book, tap the speaker icon or menu entry for TTS; check your model is on the TTS list, format support (EPUB/PDF), and that the robotic English voice is tolerable to you

Real e-ink + real read-aloud in one pocket device — voices are flat but the e-ink is lovely.

Onyx Boox — TTS on an Android e-ink screen

  • Platform: Boox Android e-readers/tablets
  • Offline: Yes — offline voices; install Android TTS apps like anywhere else
  • What it checks: Open a book and tap the speaker icon, or install a free TTS engine in Android settings; check voice packs for offline use and that e-ink refresh doesn’t stutter audio

The flexible one — a full Android e-reader, so any free TTS app on this list is one install away.

5. Bonus: free listening that isn’t synthetic

Not text-to-speech, but if the goal is “read with my ears for free,” these belong on the shelf.

Libby — free audiobooks & e-books from your library

  • Platform: iOS, Android, Kindle pairing, web
  • Offline: Partial — downloads for offline listening are supported
  • What it checks: Library card access, whether your library offers both audiobooks and e-books, hold queues, loan periods (usually 7–21 days), and offline download in the app

Professional human narration, completely free with a library card — the best listener deal available.

Human narration, not TTS

hoopla — audiobooks, comics & courses from many libraries

  • Platform: iOS, Android, web, some TVs/car systems
  • Offline: Yes — in-app downloads on supported mediums
  • What it checks: Whether your library licenses hoopla, monthly borrow limits, whether an item is “instant” (no holds) or flex (with waiting), and offline download availability

A second library card in app form — instant borrows, no holds line, no fees.

Human narration, not TTS
